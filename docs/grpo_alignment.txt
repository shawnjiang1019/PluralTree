GRPO ALIGNMENT PHASE — DESIGN
=============================

Goal. After the frozen-LLM + scout retrieval phase, train a policy that produces
pluralistic answers by RL, so the model LEARNS when to pluralize and how far,
instead of relying on a fixed injected prompt. GRPO (group-relative policy
optimization): sample a GROUP of answers per question, reward each, advantage =
(reward - group mean) / group std, policy-gradient update. No value network.

Why this phase exists (what the eval taught us)
-----------------------------------------------
v4/v5 OvertonBench + the pole-collapse study (docs/framing_hurts.png) showed the
injected prompt is a blunt instrument: it collapses the answer onto the two
injected poles (on-pole similarity 0.334 -> 0.389, corr(attraction, dcoverage)
= -0.31), and it hurts most where the model was already broad (dcov -0.45 for
baseline >= 0.8). The `route` condition tries to fix this with a smarter prompt;
GRPO is the principled version — make "commit on consensus, pluralize on genuine
disagreement, cover the real positions and no more" a learned behavior with a
reward, not a paragraph of instructions the model may or may not follow.

Reward — GROUNDED IN THE GRAPH, not the OvertonBench judge
----------------------------------------------------------
Leakage rule: the 60 OvertonBench questions + their human ratings are HELD-OUT
EVAL. RL must never train on them or on a reward derived from them. So the RL
reward is grounded in the graph's own subgroup answer distributions, which are
independent of the eval set:

  positions_from_subtree(graph, anchor): walk the anchor's subtree to its opinion
  leaves; each leaf is a subgroup's distribution over the SAME survey options.
  Aggregate to a population distribution over positions: prevalence(option) =
  mean subgroup probability of that option. This is the pluralistic target — how
  much of the population actually holds each view — and it is exactly the "full
  distribution" the content-fix would inject, reused here as ground truth.

  coverage_reward(response, positions) has three terms (alignment/reward.py):
    recall_w    prevalence-weighted fraction of real positions the response
                expresses (semantic match, held-out mpnet embedder — NOT the
                scout's MiniLM, to avoid rewarding retrieval-shaped text).
    precision   fraction of response units that map to SOME real position —
                penalizes padding / hedging / invented positions.
    verbosity   penalty for expressing MORE positions than the distribution
                supports. target = effective #positions = exp(entropy(prevalence)).
                Consensus (entropy ~ 0, target ~ 1): enumerating 4 views is
                punished — this is the `route` "commit on consensus" lesson made
                into a gradient. Contested (target ~ 3): covering 3 is rewarded.

  reward = recall_w - L_prec*(1 - precision) - L_verb*max(0, n_expr - target)/target
  clamped to [0, 1]. Anti-hacking is built in: pure enumeration loses on
  verbosity + precision; pure brevity loses on recall; copying the injected poles
  gains nothing the full-distribution recall doesn't already credit.

Policy & rollouts
-----------------
The eval reasoner (Qwen 72B AWQ) is quantized and NOT trainable. The RL policy is
a LoRA adapter over a TRAINABLE bf16 base (default Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct). Rollouts:
G samples per prompt at temperature ~1.0 via the trainer's generation (TRL colocated
vLLM). The scout retrieval is baked into the prompt at DATASET-BUILD time
(rollout_dataset.build_prompts), so the policy optimizes over already-injected
prompts and the graph encoder / scout stay frozen — this phase trains only the
reasoner's behavior given retrieval, not the retrieval itself.

Train / eval split
------------------
Training questions come from the graph's survey questions (q: nodes), NOT the 60.
build_prompts takes eval_holdout_ids (the OvertonBench question_ids) and refuses to
emit any prompt whose question maps to a held-out id. OvertonBench (job_overton_eval)
is run before and after GRPO as the external, un-optimized metric.

Open decisions (not yet fixed)
------------------------------
- base model size (7B default; 14B if reward signal is too noisy at 7B).
- reward thresholds (thr, L_prec, L_verb) — tune on a held-in slice before scaling.
- whether to add a KL-to-baseline term beyond TRL's default kl_coef to stop the
  policy drifting from fluent language while chasing coverage.
- reward is single-turn; a per-position judge (like judge_overtonbench) is a
  higher-fidelity but far more expensive alternative if the embedding match is
  too loose.
