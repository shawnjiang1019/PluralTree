\noindent
Companion to \texttt{methodology.tex}. That document defines the method; this one

reported number means what it appears to mean. Each section exists because the
corresponding confusion actually occurred and cost a run.

%==============================================================================
\section{Notation}
%==============================================================================

$Q$ is a set of questions. For $x \in Q$, $W(x)$ is its set of human viewpoint
clusters and $F(x)$ the forks the scout retrieves. A condition $c$ maps $x$ to an
answer $y_c(x)$, stochastically. $R_c(x) \subseteq W(x)$ is the set of clusters
the judge marks covered by $y_c(x)$, and
\[
  \cov_c(x) \;=\; |R_c(x)| \,/\, |W(x)|.
\]
Conditions come in two families: the \emph{control} $C$ (typically
\textsc{baseline}, no retrieval) and \emph{treatments} $T$ that consume $F(x)$.

%==============================================================================
\section{Three measurement operators, and why they are not interchangeable}
%==============================================================================

Three quantities in this project all read as ``did the content get through'' and
measure different things. Conflating them produced two wrong conclusions.

\begin{definition}[Coverage]
$\cov_c(x)$ above: a \emph{judge} decides, per cluster, whether the holders of
that viewpoint would say the answer represented them. Threshold on a predicted
1--5 rating.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Usage]
For injected positions $p \in P(x)$ and answer units $u \in U(y)$,
\[
  \usg_\theta(y, x) \;=\; \frac{1}{|P(x)|}
  \sum_{p \in P(x)} \1\!\left[\max_{u \in U(y)} \cos(e(p), e(u)) \ge \theta\right].
\]
A \emph{surface} measure: did text resembling $p$ appear. It is correlational ---
the model may have produced that unit regardless --- and has no chance floor
unless one is computed by permuting $x$ against $y$ from other questions.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Retention]
For drafts $\{y_i\}$ with deduplicated deep units $D$, and merged answer
$\hat y$,
\[
  \ret_\theta(\hat y, \{y_i\}) \;=\; \frac{1}{|D|}
  \sum_{d \in D} \1\!\left[\max_{u \in U(\hat y)} \cos(e(d), e(u)) \ge \theta\right].
\]
Measures whether \emph{compression} destroyed content, not whether the content
was ever correct or scored.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The dissociation is empirical, not hypothetical]
Measured: $\usg_{0.35} \approx 0.61$ with no coverage gain; $\ret_{0.55}$ median
$1.00$ while $\cov$ fell far below control. Surface presence, faithful
preservation, and scored coverage move independently. A claim about one
transfers to another only with an argument, never by default.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Losslessness is not judge-invariant]
Concatenating drafts is lossless in text: $\ret = 1$ by construction. It is not
lossless in coverage, because the judge scores a response \emph{as a whole} --- a
good draft appended to two poor ones is rated as a mostly-poor response. So
$\ret(\hat y) = 1 \;\not\Rightarrow\; \cov(\hat y) \ge \max_i \cov(y_i)$.
\end{remark}

\section{Abstention and attenuation}
%==============================================================================

When the scout resolves no anchor, $F(x) = \emptyset$ and the multi-draft
condition keeps only its uninjected draft, so $T$ reduces \emph{exactly} to $C$
in construction. Let $Q = Q_r \cup Q_a$ split $Q$ into resolved and abstained,
with abstention rate $\pi = |Q_a| / |Q|$.

\begin{proposition}[Attenuation]
\label{prop:atten}
If $\E[\cov_T(x) - \cov_C(x)] = 0$ for all $x \in Q_a$, then
\[
  \E\big[\hat\delta_{\text{pooled}}\big] \;=\; (1 - \pi)\, \delta_r,
  \qquad
  \delta_r \;=\; \E_{x \in Q_r}\!\left[\cov_T(x) - \cov_C(x)\right].
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\hat\delta_{\text{pooled}} = |Q|^{-1}\sum_{x \in Q}(\cov_T - \cov_C)$. The
$Q_a$ terms have zero mean, leaving $|Q_r|/|Q| \cdot \delta_r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Power loss is milder than attenuation suggests]
Pooling attenuates the numerator by $(1-\pi)$ but also adds $|Q_a|$
observations. If the per-question variance is comparable on both subsets
($\sigma_a \approx \sigma_r = \sigma$), then
$\mathrm{SE}_{\text{pooled}} \approx \sigma/\sqrt{|Q|}$ against
$\mathrm{SE}_r = \sigma/\sqrt{|Q_r|}$, so
\[
  \frac{t_{\text{pooled}}}{t_r}
  \;=\; (1-\pi)\,\frac{\mathrm{SE}_r}{\mathrm{SE}_{\text{pooled}}}
  \;=\; (1-\pi)\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\pi}} \;=\; \sqrt{1-\pi}.
\]
At $\pi = 0.29$ (INFINITY-CHAT): the effect estimate shrinks $29\%$ but the
$t$-statistic only $16\%$. Pooling is therefore \emph{biased} rather than merely
underpowered --- the reported effect size is wrong, not just noisy.
\end{remark}

\noindent
\textbf{Consequence.} Any condition that can abstain must record whether it did,
per row, and the resolved subset must be reported separately. Measured
abstention rates: \textsc{persona\_merge} $21/180$; \textsc{merge\_v2} on
INFINITY-CHAT $29/100$.

\section{Ablation validity}
%==============================================================================

An ablation compares $T$ against $T'$ differing in one component. If the
component did not actually differ, the run produces a complete result table that
means nothing: a tie is then \emph{uninformative} rather than evidence of no
effect. Validity conditions are therefore preconditions on the run, checked
before its scores are read.

Let $n_c(x) = |F_c(x)|$ and $\bar\Wass_c(x)$ the mean divergence of selected
forks.

\begin{definition}[Valid selection ablation]
\textsc{divrand} replaces the rank key $s(u,v)$ by a deterministic uniform draw
over the \emph{same} candidate pool. It is valid iff, over $Q$,
\[
  \text{(i) } \E[n_{T'}] = \E[n_{T}],
  \qquad
  \text{(ii) } \E[\bar\Wass_{T'}] < \E[\bar\Wass_{T}],
  \qquad
  \text{(iii) } \E[\bar{\mathrm{rel}}_{T'}] \approx \E[\bar{\mathrm{rel}}_{T}].
\]
\end{definition}

(i) is structural: both modes rank one pool and truncate at $k$, so counts cannot
diverge. (ii) is forced --- $T$ takes the argmax, so a uniform draw over the same
pool must sit below it; equality means the draw selected the same pairs and the
ablation is inert. (iii) distinguishes this from the \emph{content} control,
whose whole purpose is to be \emph{less} relevant; applying that control's check
here would flag correct behaviour as failure.

\begin{definition}[Valid hierarchy ablation]
\textsc{flat} retrieves by similarity over opinion leaves with no descent. It is
valid iff it abstains on the same questions: $F_{T'}(x) = \emptyset \iff
F_T(x) = \emptyset$, enforced by applying the identical relevance gate $\tau$.
Without it the arms differ in \emph{which questions get injected at all}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:atten} applies asymmetrically --- confounding the
hierarchy test with abstention.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Valid content control]
\textsc{rand} substitutes structurally matched forks from an unrelated anchor. It
is valid iff $\E[n_{T'}] = \E[n_T]$, rendered lengths match, and relevance is
\emph{lower}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[This is not hypothetical either]
A content control violated all three conditions for three consecutive runs
($1.00$ forks against $4.85$, and a dispatch gap routing it to a different merge
algorithm). Its reported tie was read as evidence about fork content; it was
evidence about nothing.
\end{remark}

%==============================================================================
\section{Identification: which contrast measures which stage}
%==============================================================================

The pipeline factors into \textsc{retrieve} $\to$ \textsc{deliver} $\to$
\textsc{compress}. A change in $\cov$ can originate in any of them, and a null in
one stage can be produced by a later stage destroying what an earlier stage
supplied. Each contrast below holds two stages fixed.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
contrast & identifies & holds fixed \\
\midrule
$T_{c=0.5}$ vs.\ $T_{c=0}$ & embedding geometry & graph, content, delivery, merge \\
$T$ vs.\ \textsc{divrand} & fork \emph{selection} & graph, anchors, volume, merge \\
$T$ vs.\ \textsc{flat} & \emph{hierarchy} & volume, rendering, gate, merge \\
$T$ vs.\ \textsc{rand} & fork \emph{content} & volume, structure, merge \\
$\alpha$ sweep (CAD) & delivery channel strength & prompt, sampler, RNG \\
$7$B vs.\ $72$B & generator capacity & questions, drafts, retrieval \\
$\ret$, guard on/off & compression fidelity & drafts \\
graph swap (ATP/ISSP) & corpus & eval, judge, questions, conditions \\
task swap (Overton/VITAL) & construct + scorer & graph, embeddings, conditions \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}[Graph swap and task swap are orthogonal]
Changing the retrieval corpus while holding the eval fixed, and changing the eval
while holding the corpus fixed, answer different questions. Neither is ``more
data'' for the other: a replication of a conclusion on a different construct with
a different scorer does not tighten the original confidence interval.
\end{remark}

\section{The noise floor from an invariant condition}
%==============================================================================

\textsc{baseline} uses no retrieval. It is therefore \emph{invariant} to every
retrieval-side manipulation: embedding curvature, selection rule, hierarchy, fork
content. Any difference in $\overline{\cov}_C$ between two arms of such an
ablation is pure run-to-run noise, and supplies a free scale against which the
treatment delta is read.

\begin{proposition}[Noise floor]
For arms $g_1, g_2$ differing only in a retrieval-side component,
$\E[\overline{\cov}_C^{(g_1)} - \overline{\cov}_C^{(g_2)}] = 0$, so
$|\overline{\cov}_C^{(g_1)} - \overline{\cov}_C^{(g_2)}|$ estimates the
sampling scale of a single arm's mean.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
Measured, each a single draw of the drift and therefore itself noisy:
$|0.5089 - 0.4951| = 0.0138$ at 72B with one rollout per question;
$|0.5024 - 0.5039| = 0.0015$ for the same two arms at three rollouts; and
$|0.4277 - 0.3913| = 0.0364$ at 7B with one rollout. The floor depends on
generator and rollout count, so it must be read from the run in question rather
than quoted as one project-wide number. A treatment delta of the same magnitude
as its own run's baseline drift is not distinguishable from zero regardless of
its nominal $p$-value.

\section{Inference}
%==============================================================================

\paragraph{Paired bootstrap over questions.} For conditions $c_1, c_2$ on shared
$Q$,
\[
  \hat\delta = \frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{x \in Q}\big[\cov_{c_1}(x) - \cov_{c_2}(x)\big],
\]
with interval and two-sided $p$ from $10^4$ resamples \emph{of questions}. Since
resampling is over questions, between-question heterogeneity sets the floor on
interval width: additional rollouts per question reduce only the within-question
component, and cannot resolve an effect that question heterogeneity obscures.
This is why $n = 60$, not rollout count, is the binding constraint.

\paragraph{Difference of differences.} For arms $g_1, g_2$,
$\widehat{\Delta\Delta} = \hat\delta_{g_1} - \hat\delta_{g_2}$, bootstrapped
paired over the same $Q$.

\begin{remark}[The overlap fallacy]
$\hat\delta_{g_1}$ significant and $\hat\delta_{g_2}$ not does \emph{not}
establish $\delta_{g_1} \ne \delta_{g_2}$. The geometry ablation at one rollout
gave $+0.0136$ ($p = 0.62$) against $+0.0707$ ($p = 0.014$) --- an apparently
decisive contrast whose difference of differences was $p = 0.094$. At three
rollouts the same arms gave $+0.0402$ and $+0.0453$, with
$\widehat{\Delta\Delta} = +0.0051$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0402, +0.0573]$, $p = 0.88$:
a well-powered null. The one-rollout picture was an artifact of within-question
noise, in the direction that looked like a finding.
\end{remark}

\section{Union, oracle and gain}
%==============================================================================

With $R_c(x)$ the covered set:
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{union}(x)  &= \Big|\textstyle\bigcup_{c \in S} R_c(x)\Big| \big/ |W(x)|, \\
  \mathrm{oracle}(x) &= \max_{c \in S} |R_c(x)| \big/ |W(x)|, \\
  \mathrm{gain}      &= \overline{\mathrm{union}} - \max_{c \in S} \overline{\cov_c}.
\end{align*}

$\mathrm{union} \ge \mathrm{oracle} \ge \max_c \overline{\cov_c}$ holds by
construction: adding an answer cannot remove a covered cluster, and a union is at
least as large as the better single set. Note $\mathrm{oracle}$ is a mean of
per-question maxima while $\max_c \overline{\cov_c}$ is a maximum of means, so
that inequality is arithmetic, not empirical.

\begin{remark}[Gain measures difference, not quality]
Gain is unrealised headroom, and is interpretable only jointly with the level:
high level with low gain is the target; high level with high gain is good content
under lossy compression; low level with high gain finds distinct viewpoints and
delivers few. Critically, \emph{low gain has two opposite causes} --- an arm that
already delivers everything it finds, and an arm that finds nothing new --- which
gain alone cannot separate. Arms equal in $\overline{\cov}$ to within $0.006$
have differed by $2.4\times$ in gain.
\end{remark}

\section{Scorer validity}
%==============================================================================

A scorer used for \emph{relative} comparison need not be unbiased, only
\emph{consistently} biased across the arms compared. This licenses far less than
it appears to.

\begin{definition}[Admissible comparison]
A contrast $c_1$ vs.\ $c_2$ under scorer $S$ is admissible if $S$'s bias is
constant in every respect on which $c_1$ and $c_2$ differ.
\end{definition}

\noindent
Length is the binding case. If $S$ is length-sensitive --- documented for
NLI-style coverage scorers, and unmeasured for the embedding-match reward ---
then any contrast whose arms differ systematically in answer length is
inadmissible. Injected answers run roughly $5\times$ baseline's length, so
$\textsc{baseline}$ vs.\ a merge condition is inadmissible without a length
control, while merge-vs-merge contrasts (geometry, selection, hierarchy) are
admissible, having matched length distributions by construction.

\begin{remark}[Validation level must match use level --- and must be this judge's]
Two separate gaps. First, a judge validated for \emph{ranking systems} is not
thereby validated for \emph{per-question} agreement, and every within-question
comparison in this project operates below the ranking level. Second, and more
basic: the benchmark's reported $\rho = 0.88$ (rank correlation over 8 reference
models) is the \emph{benchmark authors'} judge. Reproduced with this project's
judge, aggregate agreement is $\rho = 0.167$ ($0.357$ in an earlier run with too
few participants per question) and within-participant $\rho = 0.059$. Whether
that is poor depends on the human split-half ceiling at the same level, which has
not been measured --- ``$0.167$ against $0.88$'' can look conclusive when the
achievable ceiling sits far below $0.88$. Until the ceiling is known the judge is
unvalidated at every level, and its unmeasured reliability bounds any concordance
a reward can reach against it.
\end{remark}
