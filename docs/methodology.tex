%==============================================================================
\section{Problem formulation}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Overton coverage}

Let $x$ be a question on which a population holds several distinct viewpoints.
Let $W(x) = \{w_1,\dots,w_{m}\}$ be the set of viewpoint clusters that real
respondents to $x$ hold. A language model $M$ produces a single answer
$y = M(x)$. We say $y$ \emph{covers} cluster $w$ when a majority of the people
holding $w$ judge that $y$ represents their view.

\begin{definition}[Overton coverage]
\label{def:coverage}
\[
  \cov(y, x) \;=\; \frac{1}{|W(x)|}\sum_{w \in W(x)} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\,w \in R(y)\,\right],
  \qquad
  R(y) = \{\, w \in W(x) : \bar{\rho}_w(y) \ge 4 \,\},
\]
where $\bar{\rho}_w(y)$ is the mean 1--5 representation rating of $y$ by
holders of $w$. The benchmark score is the mean of $\cov$ over questions.
\end{definition}

Coverage is \emph{unweighted} over clusters: a minority viewpoint counts the
same as a majority one. This is what distinguishes the construct from
distributional pluralism, which would weight by prevalence.

\subsection{The single-answer constraint and its decomposition}

The task requires \emph{one} answer. Any system producing $y$ must therefore
(i) identify the viewpoints, (ii) get them into the generator, and (iii) fit
them into one response. We name these stages and treat them separately
throughout, because they fail separately:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
stage & object & diagnostic \\
\midrule
\textsc{retrieve} & a set of viewpoint contrasts $F(x)$ & union gain over a no-retrieval answer \\
\textsc{deliver}  & a conditioning channel for $F(x)$ & coverage with vs.\ without $F(x)$ at fixed $M$ \\
\textsc{compress} & one $y$ from several drafts & coverage of $y$ vs.\ union of its drafts \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A gain in the final score can originate at any stage; a null can be produced by
any stage destroying what an earlier one supplied. Every experiment below is
designed to vary one stage with the other two held fixed.

%==============================================================================
\section{The viewpoint graph}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Structure}

The graph is a rooted tree $T = (V, E)$ built from population survey data. Each
survey question $q$ is a node; beneath it, one node per demographic
\emph{axis} (age, party, religion, \dots); beneath each axis, one \emph{opinion
leaf} $\ell$ per subgroup, carrying that subgroup's empirical answer
distribution
\[
  p_\ell \in \simplex^{m_q - 1}, \qquad \textstyle\sum_o p_\ell(o) = 1,
\]
over the $m_q$ options of $q$. Questions are grouped under topic and subtopic
nodes. In the primary graph (Pew American Trends Panel; 1{,}492 questions, 58
subpopulations) the topic and subtopic layers are \emph{induced} by $k$-means
over sentence embeddings of question text; they are not native to the survey.
This matters for the geometry ablation (\S\ref{sec:ablations}).

\subsection{Embedding}

Every node $v \in V$ is embedded as $\phi(v) \in \ball^d_c$, the Poincar\'e ball
of curvature $-c$, by a tree-recurrent encoder with gated injection of node
text features. The embedding is trained on link prediction over the tree's
edges and reported with structural-fidelity metrics: rank correlation of depth
with radius, of tree distance with geodesic distance, ancestor-AUC, and a
sibling-separation ratio. Setting $c = 0$ recovers Euclidean space with the
same encoder; this is the geometry ablation.

%==============================================================================
\section{Retrieval: the divergence scout}
%==============================================================================

Given $x$, the scout returns a small set of \emph{forks}. A fork is a triple
$(a, u, v)$: an anchor node $a$ and two of its children $u, v$ whose subtrees
disagree.

\paragraph{Relevance.} Let $e(x)$ be a sentence embedding of $x$ and $t(v)$ the
same embedding of node $v$'s text. Define $\rel(v) = \cos(e(x), t(v))$.

\paragraph{Anchors.} $A(x)$ is the set of the $n_a$ most relevant internal nodes
after lexical matching on entities named in $x$.

\paragraph{Gated point clouds.} For a child $u$ of an anchor, let
$S(u) = \{\, \phi(z) : z \in \mathrm{subtree}(u),\ \rel(z) \ge \tau \,\}$,
truncated to at most $n_s$ nodes, with a floor of $n_{\min}$ so that a branch
is never emptied. Each point carries mass
$\mu_u(z) \propto \exp(\rel(z)/T)$.

\paragraph{Divergence.} For siblings $u, v$ under $a$,
\[
  \Wass(u, v) \;=\; \min_{\gamma \in \Pi(\mu_u, \mu_v)}
  \sum_{z \in S(u)} \sum_{z' \in S(v)} \gamma(z, z')\, d_c\!\big(\phi(z), \phi(z')\big),
\]
the optimal-transport distance between the two subtrees' embedding clouds under
the hyperbolic ground metric $d_c$. Under $c=0$, $d_c$ is Euclidean.

\paragraph{Scoring and selection.} Each candidate pair is scored
\[
  s(u, v) \;=\; \max\!\big(\bar{\rel}(u,v),\, 0\big)^{\alpha} \cdot \Wass(u, v),
  \qquad
  \bar{\rel}(u,v) = \tfrac12\big(\overline{\rel}(S(u)) + \overline{\rel}(S(v))\big),
\]
and the scout returns $F(x) = \topk_{(u,v)} s(u,v)$ over all anchors. Defaults:
$\tau = 0.25$, $\alpha = 1$, $k = 5$, $n_a = 4$, $n_s = 32$, $T = 0.1$.

\begin{remark}
Selection is the only place the divergence enters. Everything downstream sees
$F(x)$ as a set of node triples. The selection ablation therefore replaces
$s(u,v)$ by a deterministic hash-based uniform rank over the \emph{same}
candidate pool, so that anchors, gating, fork count and rendering are byte-
identical and only which pairs survive differs.
\end{remark}

%==============================================================================
\section{Delivery: rendering and injection}
%==============================================================================

Each fork is rendered to text by a fixed function $\rho$: a header naming the
anchor with its divergence and relevance, the two poles' option texts, and up
to $n_p$ \emph{driver pairs} $(z, z')$ from the transport plan $\gamma$ with the
largest $\gamma(z,z')\,d_c$. A \emph{full-spectrum} variant instead renders
every opinion leaf under the anchor, ordered by projection onto the
pole-to-pole axis $p_v - p_u$ in distribution space.

The context $\rho(F(x))$ is placed in the prompt with an instruction $I$. The
model generates a reasoning span, then an answer span; only the answer is
scored. This is the delivery channel that the CAD experiment
(\S\ref{sec:cad}) shows to be harmful.

%==============================================================================
\section{Compression: multi-draft merge}
%==============================================================================

\label{sec:merge}
Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(I_i, \beta_i, \sigma_i)\}_{i=1}^{n_d}$ be draft
specifications: an instruction, whether forks are injected, and whether the
full-spectrum render is used. The default is
\[
  \mathcal{D} = \big\{(\text{plain}, 0, 0),\ (\text{scout}, 1, 0),\ (\text{distributional}, 1, 1)\big\}.
\]
Drafts are generated independently,
\[
  y_i = M\big(I_i,\ x,\ \beta_i \cdot \rho_{\sigma_i}(F(x))\big),
\]
and merged by a single further call
$\hat y = M(I_{\mathrm{merge}}, x, \{y_i\})$ instructed to keep one paragraph per
distinct position and drop nothing.

\paragraph{Guard.} The merge is accepted only if it did not compress its
inputs. With $|\cdot|_w$ word count and $\pi(\cdot)$ the number of paragraphs of
at least $d_{\min}$ words,
\[
  |\hat y|_w \ge \lambda_\ell \max_i |y_i|_w,
  \qquad
  \pi(\hat y) \ge \lambda_\pi \max_i \pi(y_i),
\]
with $\lambda_\ell = \lambda_\pi = 1$. An optional \emph{semantic} check
dedupes the drafts' deep units by embedding, then requires that a fraction
$\ge \lambda_r$ of them have a unit of $\hat y$ within cosine $\theta_r$
($\lambda_r = 0.8$, $\theta_r = 0.55$). On failure the drafts are concatenated
under neutral headings, which is lossless in content by construction.

\begin{remark}
The concatenation fallback is lossless in \emph{text}, not in \emph{judged
coverage}: the judge rates a response as a whole, and a good draft appended to
two poor ones is rated as a mostly-poor response. This distinction is what the
7B scale result exposed.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Variants.} \textsc{persona\_merge} keeps $n_d$ and the single merge
call but conditions drafts 2..$n_d$ on individual subgroup vantage points
$\ell$ chosen by spreading along the pole-to-pole axis (poles first, then
widest gap), each told to commit to that view. \textsc{merge\_v2\_rand}
replaces $F(x)$ with structurally matched forks from an unrelated anchor
(depth-matched, rendered-length-matched, one per real fork).

%==============================================================================
\section{Decoding-level interventions}
%==============================================================================

\label{sec:cad}
\paragraph{Context-aware decoding.} With $\ell_{\mathrm{ctx}}$ the next-token
logits under the fork-injected prompt and $\ell_{\emptyset}$ under the same
instruction with the fork block removed,
\[
  \ell_{\mathrm{cad}}(\alpha) \;=\; (1+\alpha)\,\ell_{\mathrm{ctx}} - \alpha\,\ell_{\emptyset}
  \;=\; \ell_{\emptyset} + (1+\alpha)\,(\ell_{\mathrm{ctx}} - \ell_{\emptyset}).
\]
$\alpha$ scales the context's contribution: $\alpha > 0$ amplifies,
$\alpha \in (-1, 0)$ interpolates toward the no-context model, $\alpha = -1$
recovers it exactly. In probability space this is the geometric blend
$p \propto p_{\mathrm{ctx}}^{\,1+\alpha}\, p_{\emptyset}^{-\alpha}$.

\paragraph{Graph-guided G2.} Three streams decode in lockstep: a base stream
$z$ seeing only $x$, and two guide streams seeing prior answers, one told to
diverge and one to replicate. With $H$ the entropy of $\mathrm{softmax}(z)$,
\[
  \ell = z + \alpha_t\,(z^{+} - z^{-}), \qquad
  \alpha_t = \theta\cdot \mathbf{1}[H(z) \ge \beta].
\]
The graph variant replaces the generic ``diverge'' instruction with a named
subgroup vantage point from $F(x)$. The forks never enter the base stream; they
contribute a direction, not text.

\section{Ablations and controls}
%==============================================================================

\label{sec:ablations}
Each ablation changes exactly one term of the pipeline above.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{4.6cm}p{4.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
name & stage & what varies & what is held fixed \\
\midrule
geometry $c \in \{0.5, 0\}$ & retrieve & ground metric $d_c$, encoder curvature & training config, seed, $\mathcal{D}$, judge \\
\textsc{divrand} & retrieve & rank key $s \to$ uniform hash & anchors, gate, $k$, $\rho$, merge \\
\textsc{flat} & retrieve & no tree: leaves ranked by $\rel$ alone, gated at $\tau$ & $k$, driver count, $\rho$, merge \\
\textsc{rand} & retrieve & fork \emph{content} (unrelated anchor) & fork count, depth, length, merge \\
CAD $\alpha$ & deliver & context influence & prompt, sampler, RNG stream \\
scale $7$B vs $72$B & deliver & generator & questions, $\mathcal{D}$ \\
\textsc{sem} guard & compress & semantic check on/off & drafts, structural guard \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Manipulation checks.} An ablation whose arms do not actually differ
yields a plausible table that means nothing. Every retrieved condition therefore
records per row its selection mode, fork count, mean $\Wass$ over selected
forks, mean relevance, and the anchor pool examined. \textsc{divrand} is valid
only if its fork count equals the reference's and its mean $\Wass$ is strictly
lower (the reference takes the argmax). \textsc{rand} is valid only if fork
counts match and relevance is lower. \textsc{flat} is valid only if it abstains
on the same questions the reference abstains on. These are asserted by a job
that gates every full run.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
%==============================================================================

\paragraph{Score.} OvertonScore as in Definition~\ref{def:coverage}, with an LLM
judge predicting each participant's rating; $\bar\rho_w$ is the mean predicted
rating over the participants in $w$. The judge is validated against held-out
human ratings for \emph{ranking} reference systems; its per-cluster agreement
is not established, which bounds every within-question comparison below.

\paragraph{Paired comparison.} For conditions $c_1, c_2$ on shared questions
$Q$, the estimand is
\[
  \bar\delta = \frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{x \in Q}
  \big[\cov(y_{c_1}(x), x) - \cov(y_{c_2}(x), x)\big],
\]
with a 95\% interval and two-sided $p$ from a 10{,}000-draw bootstrap over
\emph{questions}. Because resampling is
over questions, additional rollouts per question reduce within-question noise
only; between-question heterogeneity sets the floor on interval width.

\paragraph{Difference of differences.} For an ablation with arms $g_1, g_2$,
$\Delta\Delta = \bar\delta_{g_1} - \bar\delta_{g_2}$ bootstrapped over the same
questions. Two arms that are individually significant and non-significant
against baseline have \emph{not} thereby been shown to differ.

\paragraph{Union, oracle, gain.} With $R_c(x)$ the covered set under condition
$c$:
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{union}(x)  &= \Big|\textstyle\bigcup_c R_c(x)\Big| \big/ |W(x)|, \
  \mathrm{oracle}(x) &= \max_c |R_c(x)| \big/ |W(x)|, \
  \mathrm{gain}      &= \overline{\mathrm{union}} - \max_c\, \overline{|R_c|/|W|}.
\end{align*}
Union $\ge$ oracle $\ge$ best single, by construction. Gain measures what an arm
\emph{adds} to another, not how it scores; two arms can be equal alone and
differ several-fold in gain. It is a ceiling on any merge, not a score.

\paragraph{Noise floor.} The baseline condition uses no retrieval and cannot
depend on any retrieval-side change. Its spread across otherwise identical runs
is therefore a direct estimate of run-to-run noise, and any delta of that size
is not distinguishable from zero.

\section{What is and is not established}
%==============================================================================

For calibration of the claims above (numbers from the September 2026 runs):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsc{merge\_v2} $>$ baseline replicates: $+0.039$, $+0.050$
    ($p{=}0.014$), $+0.048$ ($p{=}0.024$) across independent generations at 72B.
  \item CAD at 7B is monotone in $\alpha$ across five values
    ($0.352, 0.327, 0.099, 0.047, 0.042$ for $\alpha = -0.5..{+}0.5$; no-forks
    $0.394$): the delivery channel is harmful, and its harm is tunable.
  \item Hyperbolic vs.\ Euclidean: the hyperbolic encoder fits the tree markedly
    better (tree-distance $\rho$ $0.64$ vs $0.29$; sibling ratio $0.65$ vs
    $0.08$) and yields no coverage gain ($+0.014$, $p{=}0.62$) where Euclidean
    does ($+0.071$, $p{=}0.014$); $\Delta\Delta = +0.057$, $p{=}0.094$. No
    evidence the geometry contributes; not established that it hurts.
  \item At 7B, \textsc{merge\_v2} falls $0.17$--$0.26$ below baseline; the merge
    retains its drafts' content (median retention $1.0$), so the loss is in
    draft quality, not compression.
  \item Divergence selection (\textsc{divrand}) and hierarchy (\textsc{flat})
    are untested at time of writing; the content control (\textsc{rand}) was
    confounded in every prior run and is being rerun.
  \item Reward--judge concordance within question is $0.15$ against chance
    $0.5$; the reward scores survey positions, the judge scores participant
    clusters, and no threshold reconciles them.
\end{itemize}
