# Findings, 2026-09-20

The headline result reverses under a better-calibrated judge, and the cause is
measurable: answer length. Everything else here is secondary to that.

Supersedes parts of [`findings_2026_09_15.md`](findings_2026_09_15.md) (§1, §12)
and [`established_claims.tex`](established_claims.tex). Job-by-job record in
[`jobs-running.txt`](jobs-running.txt).

## 1. The headline reverses under a different judge

Same 60 questions, same stored answers, two judges:

| condition | Qwen2.5-72B-AWQ | Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.4899 | 0.3899 |
| merge_v2 | **0.5589** | 0.2679 |
| merge_v2_flat | 0.5504 | 0.2711 |

Paired against baseline under the new judge: merge_v2 **−0.122**
(95% CI [−0.168, −0.074], p < 0.0001), flat −0.119 ([−0.164, −0.074]). Under the
old judge merge_v2 was **+0.069** ([+0.024, +0.113], p = 0.003). The intervals do
not overlap: a 0.19 swing in opposite directions on identical text.

## 2. Why: length, and where the benchmark was calibrated

**The judges disagree about length.**

| | ρ(words, coverage) | shortest 25% (55 w) | longest 25% (778 w) |
|---|---|---|---|
| old judge | +0.079 | 0.466 | 0.553 |
| new judge | −0.244 | 0.380 | 0.228 |
| humans (reference models) | −0.004 | — | — |

**And the human calibration covers a narrow band the systems never enter.**

| | p5 | p50 | p95 | max | within human p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| human-rated responses (n=28,992) | 66 | 88 | 97 | 106 | — |
| baseline | 30 | 65 | 116 | — | 85.6% |
| merge_v2 | 448 | 672 | 879 | — | **0.0%** |
| merge_v2_flat | 464 | 667 | 896 | — | **0.0%** |

Humans show no length effect (−0.004 pooled, +0.019 within question) but only
ever rated 66–106 word answers. merge_v2 answers are ~7x longer than the longest
response any human rated, so every judge verdict on them is extrapolation, and
two reasonable judges extrapolate oppositely.

## 3. Which judge to believe: the new one, on the axis that matters

Both validated against the same 300 held-out human ratings:

| | old judge | new judge | human ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| per-rating MAE | 0.527 | **0.523** | 0.592 |
| per-rating spearman | **0.653** | 0.626 | 0.673 |
| corr(rating, length) vs human −0.017 | — | **−0.017** | — |
| length-strata bias | +0.147, +0.147, +0.280, **+0.333** | +0.000, −0.053, −0.067, +0.080 | — |
| P(rating ≥ 4) vs human 0.650 | 0.747 | **0.647** | — |
| within-participant spearman | +0.059 | −0.014 | — |
| 8-model ranking spearman | 0.167 / 0.357 | 0.238, CI [−0.97, +0.92] | 0.574 (split-half) |

Per-rating accuracy is a wash; both sit near the human ceiling. The separation is
on the two axes that decide the headline:

- **Length bias.** The old judge over-rates the longest stratum by +0.333 of a
  rating point and the bias grows monotonically with length. The new judge's
  length correlation matches humans exactly.
- **The coverage threshold.** Coverage counts a cluster at mean rating >= 4. The
  old judge says >= 4 for 74.7% of ratings against humans' 65.0%; the new judge
  is at 64.7%.

So the negative verdict comes from the better-calibrated instrument on the
dimension in dispute. The 8-model ranking level cannot adjudicate anything: its
bootstrap CI spans [−0.97, +0.92], and human split-half reliability there is only
0.574.

**Both judges remain unvalidated above ~110 words.** The new judge simply has no
measured in-band length bias to extrapolate.

## 4. The length controls (running)

`len_ctrl`: `baseline, baseline_long, merge_v2, merge_v2_brief`, 3 rollouts,
scored under both judges.

- **`baseline_long`** — no retrieval, ~650 words (merge_v2's p50 is 672). Both
  arms long, so judge length bias cancels.
- **`merge_v2_brief`** — full retrieval and merge, final answer ~90 words, inside
  the human band. The only clean test of whether retrieved content helps.
  Implementation note: merge_v2's lossless guard asserts merged >= longest draft
  in words and positions, so a brief merge is flagged lossy and replaced by the
  concatenation fallback. The guard's floors are relaxed for this condition only.

Not a handicap: the benchmark's own reference models answered in ~88 words and
scored 3.7-3.8 with humans.

## 5. Selection ablations (measured with the OLD judge — now suspect)

All three perturbations of merge_v2's selection rule lose its entire gain:

| arm vs merge_v2 | delta | 95% CI | p | vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| spread | −0.085 | [−0.140, −0.031] | 0.002 | −0.016 (n.s.) |
| jsdiv | −0.059 | [−0.109, −0.010] | 0.017 | −0.021 (n.s.) |
| cover | −0.050 | [−0.106, +0.004] | 0.071 | +0.019 (n.s.) |

Manipulation checks passed on volume (4.85-4.90 forks) and, for jsdiv, on
disagreement (mean_js 0.0447 vs 0.0395). But **spread did not do what it was
built to do**: 1.67 distinct questions per row against merge_v2's 1.98 — it
changed branch granularity, not topic spread.

No measured property orders the losses: questions/row (flat injects 5.00 and
*ties*; spread 1.67 and loses), relevance (cover drops it most, −0.083, and loses
least), W, or JS. The parsimonious reading is that any substitution for
`argmax(rel^alpha * W)` returns to baseline, and the data cannot say which
property matters.

**Caveat added today:** all of these were scored by the length-biased judge on
600+ word answers, outside the validated band. They may be uninformative rather
than negative. Re-judging with the new judge is cheap and unrun.

## 6. RL gate: blocked by the instrument, not the reward

Stage 0 ran (60 questions x 8 samples, 7B base, plain prompts) and the gate
failed on the concordance check, not on headroom:

```
headroom          +0.176   PASS (union@8 0.544 vs within-answer 0.368)
flat groups        0.267   PASS (collapse was not the blocker)
judge vs itself    0.507   = CHANCE
reward concordance 0.228   (bar 0.503; ties on 51% of pairs; lambda=0 control 0.225)
```

Judged with all participants instead of 20: within 0.2835 -> union@8 0.4353
(+0.152), so the across-sample headroom is robust. But a judge that cannot rank
8 samples of ONE model above chance cannot validate any per-sample reward —
which also explains the old coverage reward (0.152), the new one (0.228), and
five failed routers. Note both judges are at chance here (+0.059 and −0.014
within-participant), so this is not fixed by the judge swap.

## 7. Geometry: both embeddings are boundary-saturated

`validate_divergence`, within-axis (the scale fork selection uses):

| | ATP | ISSP |
|---|---|---|
| geodesic vs JS | +0.755 | +0.086 |
| input features vs JS | +0.074 | +0.893 |
| mean JS (same-axis pairs) | 0.0154 | 0.0144 |

Cause: every node sits at the projection clamp, rho = 0.9999, 100% of nodes, on
both graphs. It is dimensional, not dynamical — the Tree-GRU tangent norm grows
like sqrt(d_hidden) (~7 at d=64) while `exp_map_zero` saturates past ~5. ATP
retains angular structure and still ranks disagreement; ISSP does not.

A fresh legacy ATP retrain shows saturation is also *progressive*: frac(rho>0.9)
0.965 -> 1.000 and pairwise distance sd 2.38 -> 1.75 over training, while val MRR
barely moves.

**Fix attempt 1 (scale only) failed**: ISSP retrained with `--tangent_scale -1`
still had p50 rho 0.9998, frac>0.9 0.844, and `depth_radius_rho` *fell* from
0.590 to 0.319. The reason is a feedback loop: `h_agg_tan` is the log map of the
children, whose norm diverges near the rim, so saturated children saturate the
parent and a constant factor only delays it.

**Fix attempt 2 (`--tangent_clip`)** bounds rho <= tanh(sqrt(c)*clip) regardless
of the children — 0.61 at clip 1.0, c 0.5. Implemented, not yet retrained.

Also unexplained: this legacy ATP retrain gets test MRR 0.2647, while the
codebase cites 0.456 for the ATP embedding. Resolve before either number is used.

## 8. What can be claimed now

**Weaker than yesterday.** "merge_v2 improves Overton coverage" is not currently
supportable: it holds under a judge that over-rates length and over-crosses the
coverage threshold, and reverses under one that matches humans on both. Neither
judge is validated at the lengths merge_v2 produces.

**Unchanged.** The across-sample headroom (+0.15 to +0.18), the benchmark's
inability to rank samples of one system, and the fact that no structural
component (curvature, hierarchy, divergence selection) explains anything.

**What would restore a positive claim.** `merge_v2_brief` beating `baseline`
inside the 66-106 word band, under both judges. That is the experiment now
running.

## 10. Cluster level: the mechanism survives the reversal (analysed 2026-09-23)

`cluster_overlap.py` on the three `*_q38_clusters.csv` dumps. §1–§4 compared
*mean* coverage; this compares the *sets*, which is where the mechanism lives.
330 clusters over 60 questions (5.5/question); baseline misses 2.93 of them per
question in every file.

| dump | arm | uniq/q | recovered | prev of uniq | prev of missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat | `merge_v2` | 0.25 | 8.5% | 0.274 | 0.455 |
| flat | `merge_v2_flat` | 0.32 | 10.8% | 0.257 | 0.455 |
| comp | `merge_v2_compressed` | 0.25 | 8.5% | 0.347 | 0.456 |

**The arms recover the minority end, and the new judge agrees.** `prev of uniq`
sits at 0.26–0.27 against 0.455 for what baseline missed: the clusters merge_v2
adds are roughly 60% the size of the average dropped cluster. This is the
mechanism OvertonBench exists to reward, it was never visible in the scalar
means, and it holds under the judge that reversed the headline.

**Compression preserves the covered set exactly.** `merge_v2_compressed` recovers
0.25 clusters/question at 8.5% — identical to the uncompressed arm — while its
mean score fell (§4). So the drop is the judge scoring the same viewpoints lower
for lack of depth, not the pipeline losing viewpoints. That separates two things
§1 could not: the *coverage mechanism* and the *depth premium*.

Not free, though: `prev of uniq` rises 0.274 → 0.347 under compression. What
survives the rewrite is the less-minority end of what merge_v2 finds, consistent
with the fixed-90 rewrite favouring enumerable majority positions. Still below
0.456, so the bias is attenuated rather than lost. Re-check on `compress_m`.

**The hierarchy null replicates at cluster level.** `merge_v2_flat` recovers
*more* than `merge_v2` (0.32 vs 0.25/q, 10.8% vs 8.5%) and is equally
minority-biased (0.257). Jaccard 0.619 between what the two arms add to baseline:
they largely find the same clusters. Tree structure contributes nothing here
either, now measured on sets rather than means.

**The ceiling is not a minority tail.** 155/330 (47.0%) of clusters are covered
by no arm in the flat dump, at mean relative prevalence **0.482** — at or above
the 0.455 of what baseline alone missed. These are mid-sized viewpoints, not
fringe ones, and no merge, router or reward in this family reaches them. That is
a bound on the method family, and it is a larger number than any effect measured
in this document.

Caveat: the three dumps carry different condition sets (3 arms / 2 arms /
baseline-only), so their "no arm covers" figures — 47.0%, 49.1%, 53.9% — measure
different unions and must not be read as a trend. Only the within-dump columns
compare.

**What this changes.** §8's "not currently supportable" stands for the mean-score
claim, but the set-level claim is stronger than the scalar one and was not
tested there: *the retrieved content reaches minority clusters baseline drops,
under both judges, at matched length*. Whether that converts into score is the
depth question, which is exactly what `baseline_long` and `compress_m` separate.

## 11. The gain/loss decomposition: what the net score was hiding (2026-09-23)

`cluster_overlap.py` reports only clusters an arm covers that baseline misses.
The score is `gain - loss`, and the loss column had never been computed. Adding
it reproduces every reported delta (flat +0.062 vs +0.069 reported, ISSP +0.047
vs +0.048, flat_q38 -0.126 vs -0.122, comp_q38 -0.178 vs -0.194), so the
decomposition is exact and the readings below are arithmetic, not inference.

### 11.1 Why minority recovery does not show up in the score

Under the new judge merge_v2 gains clusters at mean relative prevalence **0.274**
and loses them at **0.601**. It trades majority clusters for minority ones at
about one-for-two. OvertonBench weights every cluster equally, so that trade is
negative by construction: the recovered minority cluster earns exactly what the
dropped majority cluster cost. **The metric is prevalence-blind, and the
mechanism this project is built on is a prevalence trade.** No amount of better
retrieval fixes that; it is a property of the scoring rule.

This is the cleanest statement of the gap between what §10 measured (the arms
reach the minority end) and what §1 measured (the score fell).

### 11.2 The reversal is a threshold effect, not displacement

`flat` and `flat_q38` are the SAME responses scored by two judges:

| judge | gain/q | loss/q | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-72B-AWQ | 0.53 | 0.19 | +0.062 |
| Qwen3.8-27B | 0.28 | **1.00** | -0.126 |

Gain halved, loss rose 5x, with zero change to the text. Same pattern on the
compressed arm (-0.009 -> -0.178, loss 0.64 -> 1.30). merge_v2 covers many
positions shallowly, so when the >=4 bar is applied more strictly it has far more
marginal clusters to lose than baseline does. **The depth premium lives in the
loss column**, and it is not content displacement -- the pipeline never dropped
those viewpoints, the judge stopped counting them.

### 11.3 The selection ablations were never null

Same run, same judge, identical net scores, completely different ledgers:

| arm | gain/q | loss/q | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| `merge_v2` (spread run) | 0.53 | 0.17 | +0.065 |
| `merge_v2_cover` | **0.90** | 0.53 | +0.065 |
| `merge_v2_spread` | 0.81 | 0.60 | +0.037 |
| `merge_v2` (jsdiv run) | 0.53 | 0.12 | +0.083 |
| `merge_v2_jsdiv` | **0.83** | 0.36 | +0.083 |
| `merge_v2_divrand` | 0.69 | 0.17 | **+0.092** |
| `merge_v2_rand` (v12) | 0.81 | 0.38 | +0.077 |
| `persona_merge` (v12) | 0.90 | 0.53 | +0.065 |

`cover` recovers 70% more of what baseline missed than merge_v2 does; `jsdiv`
57% more. They tie on net only because they lose proportionally more. **"Any
substitution for argmax(rel^alpha * W) returns to baseline" (job log, WHAT IT
ADDS UP TO) is false.** The selection mechanisms move substantial mass in both
directions and cancel.

Correction to §5 and to the job log: those arms are not null, they are
differently shaped at equal net.

### 11.4 Loss is the untouched lever

Every intervention so far has targeted gain (better retrieval, better ranking,
better selection). Nothing has targeted loss. `cover`'s gain at `merge_v2`'s loss
rate is net **+0.127** -- double the largest effect ever measured in this project.

The concrete version: a merge guard that requires the final answer to retain the
positions a baseline answer would have covered, rather than only maximising
retrieved positions. Currently the merge optimises breadth over injected content
and is free to drop whatever the model would have said unaided. Untested.

### 11.5 The gain is 72B-specific

On the 7B generator merge_v2 is negative under both judges:

| file | gain/q | loss/q | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| `7b` | 0.12 | 1.65 | -0.285 |
| `7b_j72` | 0.45 | 0.80 | -0.062 |

Not recorded anywhere before this. Any claim about injection helping is a claim
about the 72B generator only, and the RL plan (7B policy) inherits this.

## 12. Weighted coverage with paired CIs: the decisive table (2026-09-23)

`cluster_overlap.py --boot 10000`, paired bootstrap over questions. `minority`
runs on n=56 (four questions have no cluster under half the largest). This
supersedes the point estimates in §11.3, which had no intervals.

### 12.1 Under the calibrated judge, the harm is majority-cluster harm

| file | arm | uniform | minority |
|---|---|---|---|
| flat_q38 | `merge_v2` | -0.109 [-0.157,-0.060] p<.001 | **-0.032 [-0.087,+0.025] p=.26** |
| flat_q38 | `merge_v2_flat` | -0.076 p=.007 | -0.009 p=.77 |
| comp_q38 | `merge_v2_compressed` | -0.184 p<.001 | -0.068 [-0.140,+0.002] p=.058 |

merge_v2 significantly hurts on the benchmark's own metric and is
**statistically indistinguishable from baseline on minority-weighted coverage**.
Injection destroys majority-cluster coverage; its effect on minority coverage is
not detectable in either direction. This is the precise version of "the benchmark
does not reward what we do" -- and note it is a null, not a win.

### 12.2 The attribution failure, with intervals

| run | `merge_v2` uniform | `merge_v2` minority | best control (minority) |
|---|---|---|---|
| v12 | +0.078 p=.003 | +0.065 p=.069 | `merge_v2_rand` +0.107 p=.009 |
| sel | +0.042 **p=.074** | +0.048 p=.035 | `merge_v2_divrand` +0.091 p=.005 |
| spread | +0.056 p=.028 | +0.081 p=.006 | `merge_v2_cover` +0.112 p=.014 |

**Superseded in part by 12.6**: the table above compares each arm to
baseline, so arm-vs-arm was inferred rather than tested. The direct
paired tests are weaker for two of the three claims.

In the `sel` run merge_v2 does not clear significance on the benchmark's own
metric while `divrand` and `flat` both reach p<.001 at roughly double the effect.
Across three independent runs, **random fork selection, random divergence ranking
and no hierarchy each match or beat the full method.** The six replications of
"merge_v2 > baseline" in the job log are real but are not evidence for the
retrieval mechanism: every control replicates them too.

### 12.3 Correction to §10

"47% of clusters covered by no arm" is judge-specific, not a property of the
method family. Under the old judge the figure is 20.0% (v12), 24.8% (sel), 23.0%
(spread); under the new judge 47.0% (flat_q38), 49.1% (comp_q38). The ceiling
tracks judge strictness. The §10 sentence calling it "the real ceiling ... a
finding about the METHOD FAMILY" overstated it and should be read with this.

### 12.4 The one direction with measured headroom

Jaccard of what each arm adds to baseline is 0.41-0.62 across every run: the arms
recover substantially DIFFERENT clusters. Their gains would compound. Together
with the across-sample headroom (+0.15 to +0.18 for union@8 vs 1, §6), the
evidence points at ensembling -- union over arms or over samples -- and not at
better ranking within one arm. It also does not require the retrieval mechanism
to be load-bearing, which given §12.2 is an advantage.

### 12.5 What can honestly be claimed

Not "PluralTree improves Overton coverage". The supportable statements are:

1. Injecting heterogeneous viewpoint text recovers clusters a plain answer
   misses (+0.04 to +0.09 uniform, replicated), **and the specific retrieval,
   geometry and hierarchy contribute nothing measurable** to that. Tested
   directly against merge_v2 (12.6): two of five control arms significantly
   beat it, three are indistinguishable, none is worse.
2. The gain is a prevalence trade: gains at relative prevalence ~0.27-0.59,
   losses at ~0.45-0.66. Uniform weighting nets it to zero by construction.
3. Under a judge calibrated to human ratings the trade is net negative on the
   benchmark's metric and null on minority-weighted coverage.
4. Coverage deltas are highly sensitive to judge strictness and to response
   length; the unreachable-cluster ceiling moves from 20% to 47% on judge choice
   alone.

That is a measurement paper about the fragility of Overton-style coverage claims,
not a method paper.

### 12.6 Direct arm-vs-arm tests (`--baseline merge_v2`)

Every CI in 12.2 is against baseline, so arm-vs-arm was inferred from two
separate comparisons. Re-running with merge_v2 as the reference tests it
directly, paired on question.

| run | arm vs `merge_v2` | uniform | minority |
|---|---|---|---|
| sel | `merge_v2_divrand` | **+0.048 [+0.009,+0.090] p=.016** | **+0.043 p=.042** |
| sel | `merge_v2_flat` | **+0.048 [+0.016,+0.083] p=.002** | **+0.042 p=.031** |
| v12 | `merge_v2_rand` | -0.006 p=.81 | +0.042 p=.11 |
| v12 | `persona_merge` | -0.004 p=.88 | -0.032 p=.46 |
| spread | `merge_v2_cover` | -0.001 p=.96 | +0.032 p=.31 |
| spread | `merge_v2_spread` | -0.050 p=.14 | -0.017 p=.67 |

Two claims made earlier today do not survive this:

* **"`merge_v2_rand` beats merge_v2 on minority coverage"** -- no. +0.042
  [-0.008,+0.102], p=.11. The +0.107 vs +0.065 gap in 12.2 is two baseline
  comparisons differenced, which is not a test.
* **"`merge_v2_cover` is the best arm"** -- no. -0.001 uniform, +0.032 minority
  (p=.31). Indistinguishable from merge_v2, and its Jaccard with `spread` is
  0.737, so it is also barely a distinct arm.

What IS established, and it is narrower and worse for the method: in the `sel`
run, **removing the hierarchy (`flat`) and randomising the divergence ranking
(`divrand`) each significantly improve on merge_v2**, on both metrics, paired,
same run. The ledger says why: `divrand` gains 0.43 clusters/q against merge_v2
while losing 0.09 -- strictly better-shaped, not a wash. Unreplicated: both come
from one run.

Of five control arms tested directly, two significantly beat merge_v2, three are
indistinguishable, **none is worse**. Together with merge_v2 > baseline in two of
three runs (v12 -0.078 p=.003, spread -0.056 p=.028, sel -0.042 p=.074 for
baseline vs merge_v2), the accurate summary is:

> Injection helps. The design choices inside the injection -- hierarchy,
> divergence ranking, relevance-weighted pair selection -- are neutral at best
> and harmful in the one run that tested them head to head.

### 12.7 The union signal, restated

With merge_v2 as reference, baseline's Jaccard against the other arms is
**0.069-0.189**: what baseline covers that merge_v2 misses is almost entirely
disjoint from what any injected arm recovers. Arm-to-arm Jaccard is 0.34-0.50.

This is the strongest headroom signal in the project and it argues for COMBINING
a plain answer with an injected one, not replacing one with the other -- which is
also the only direction that does not depend on the retrieval mechanism being
load-bearing (12.6 says it is not).

## 13. RL gate, second instrument: closed (2026-09-24)

`job_group_reward_gate.sh` on the q38 rollout dumps (job 3755906, exit 2 = FAIL).
Supersedes the old-judge gate in section 6.

| quantity | old judge | new judge (q38) |
|---|---|---|
| judge vs itself (ceiling) | 0.507 | **0.328** |
| primary concordance | 0.228 | 0.178 [0.094, 0.281] |
| lambda=0 control | -- | 0.169 |
| flat groups | 0.267 | 0.267 |
| headroom (union@8 - within) | +0.152 | **+0.1536** |

**The verdict is the same and stronger.** The calibrated judge ranks eight
rollouts of ONE model at 0.328, worse than the old judge's 0.507 and below
chance. The mechanism is visible in the tune table: tie rate 0.51-0.70. The new
judge is stricter (section 12.3: unreachable clusters 20% -> 47%), so far more
rollouts land on identical floor scores, and identical scores carry no ranking
signal. Per-sample RL is not blocked by our reward; it is blocked by the
benchmark's inability to separate samples of one system. That holds on both
instruments and is the claim to make.

Not that the reward is meaningless: `conc|sep` is 0.594-0.653, so among the pairs
it does separate it agrees with the judge about 60% of the time. There is simply
nothing trustworthy to validate it against.

**The headroom is the judge-robust quantity.** +0.152 -> +0.1536 across the judge
swap, while every arm-level coverage delta moved or reversed (section 12.1). The
across-sample gap is the most stable number in this project, and it is the same
quantity the union argument in 12.7 rests on.

**Code note.** With `self = 0.328` the bar `0.5 + 0.5*(self - 0.5)` evaluates to
0.414 -- a pass threshold below chance. Harmless here (everything failed anyway)
but the bar should floor at 0.5, or a sufficiently broken judge yields a passing
gate.

## 14. All five ablation runs re-judged: the injection result inverts (2026-09-24)

`cluster_overlap.py --baseline merge_v2 --boot 10000` on the q38 re-judges of
every archived run. Positive delta = the arm BEATS merge_v2.

### 14.1 Baseline beats merge_v2 on the benchmark's own metric, 4 of 5 runs

| run | baseline - merge_v2 (uniform) | minority |
|---|---|---|
| sel | +0.075 [+0.016,+0.137] p=.015 | +0.011 p=.73 |
| spread | +0.108 [+0.037,+0.178] p=.003 | +0.007 p=.85 |
| v11 | +0.084 [+0.017,+0.151] p=.016 | +0.028 p=.49 |
| jsdiv | +0.107 [+0.046,+0.166] p=.002 | +0.018 p=.62 |
| issp | +0.040 [-0.008,+0.088] p=.098 | +0.015 p=.63 |

Six replications of "merge_v2 > baseline" under the old judge become four

### 14.2 The minority null replicates five times with no exceptions

Every minority-weighted comparison above is null (p=.49 to .85). Section 12.1 was
a single run; it now holds on five independent runs. **Injection destroys
majority-cluster coverage and does nothing detectable to minority coverage.**
The consistency here is stronger than any effect in this document, and it is the
result most worth building the writeup around.

### 14.3 RETRACTION of 12.6

The central claim of 12.6 -- that `divrand` and `flat` significantly beat

| `merge_v2_divrand` | +0.048 p=.016 | -0.008 p=.74 |

It was a single-run, single-judge result and it is withdrawn. The surviving and

### 14.4 NEW: `persona_merge` beats merge_v2, and possibly baseline

| persona_merge vs merge_v2 (v11, q38) | delta | p |
|---|---|---|
| uniform | **+0.144 [+0.072,+0.215]** | **.000** |
| minority | +0.084 [-0.017,+0.182] | .096 |
| invprev | +0.112 [+0.029,+0.193] | .008 |

At 0.500 it also sits above baseline's 0.440 in the same run, which would make it

What persona_merge is: a multi-draft merge over PERSONAS. No KG retrieval, no

### 14.5 Where this leaves the claim ladder

Section 12.5 said "a measurement paper about the fragility of Overton-style
coverage claims, not a method paper". That still stands, sharpened:

1. Under a judge calibrated to human ratings, retrieval-augmented merging is
   WORSE than a plain answer on OvertonBench coverage (4/5 runs, p<=.016).
2. The damage is confined to majority clusters; minority coverage is unchanged
   (5/5 null). The uniform metric cannot express this distinction.
3. No component of the method -- hierarchy, curvature, divergence ranking,
   anchor spread, coverage-greedy selection, fork relevance -- is
   distinguishable from its ablation on either judge.
4. The one positive signal (14.4, 15) uses the SAME graph and scout but
   diversifies drafts by subgroup instead of by instruction. It points
   away from merge_v2's use of the graph, not away from the graph.
5. The across-sample headroom (+0.15) is the only judge-robust quantity
   (section 13).

## 15. `persona_merge` beats baseline on BOTH judges (2026-09-24)

The direct test 14.4 asked for. `cluster_overlap.py --clusters
overton_scores_v11_q38_clusters.csv --boot 10000`, baseline as reference.

| arm vs baseline | old judge (v12_cl) | q38 |
|---|---|---|
| `merge_v2` | +0.078 [+0.025,+0.135] p=.003 | **-0.084 [-0.151,-0.017] p=.016** |
| `merge_v2_rand` | +0.072 [+0.006,+0.141] p=.036 | **-0.087 [-0.150,-0.019] p=.014** |
| `persona_merge` | +0.075 [+0.027,+0.126] p=.002 | **+0.060 [+0.008,+0.111] p=.024** |

**`persona_merge` is the only arm in this project that survives the judge swap.**
Everything else inverts; it moves +0.075 -> +0.060 and stays significant. Given
that judge sensitivity is what destroyed every other result (section 14.1), this
is the property that matters most.

Minority +0.056 (p=.098) and invprev +0.062 (p=.058) both trend positive without
clearing significance, so the win is on the benchmark's own metric and does not
depend on our reweighting.

### 15.1 It is a different mechanism, not a better-tuned one

| arm | gain/q | loss/q | prev gain | prev loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `merge_v2` | 0.37 | 0.91 | 0.418 | 0.575 |
| `merge_v2_rand` | 0.36 | 0.87 | 0.295 | 0.603 |
| `persona_merge` | **0.72** | **0.36** | 0.499 | 0.504 |

merge_v2 TRADES: sheds 0.91 clusters to gain 0.37, and sheds more prevalent ones
than it gains. persona_merge ADDS: double the gain, less than half the loss, with
gain and loss at the same prevalence (0.499 vs 0.504). It is not making the
majority-for-minority trade that section 11.1 identified as the structural
problem, which is why it wins on uniform weighting rather than needing a
reweighting to look good.

**Jaccard(persona_merge, merge_v2) = 0.033** -- near-total disjointness. What
persona recovers is almost entirely not what the retrieval pipeline recovers.
(vs `rand`: 0.088.)

### 15.2 What it means for the project

**CORRECTED 2026-09-24.** An earlier version of this section claimed
persona_merge uses "no KG, no scout, no hyperbolic embedding". That was wrong,
read from the results rather than the code. `_persona_merge_answer` calls
`pick_personas(forks[0], graph, n-1)`: it takes the SCOUT's top fork and selects
subgroups along that anchor's opinion spectrum from the GRAPH (both poles first,
then the widest gap), and `persona_context` feeds each draft that subgroup's
actual survey distribution.

It also uses the same `merge_drafts` call, the same `MergeConfig` and the same
length floor as merge_v2, and its first draft is the identical plain baseline.
The docstring states the design: compute-matched, "the only difference is the
source of draft-to-draft variation".

So the contrast is narrow and clean:

* `merge_v2` -- three drafts see the SAME fork block, varied by INSTRUCTION
  (plain / scout / distributional).
* `persona_merge` -- three drafts see DIFFERENT subgroups from the graph, varied
  by CONTENT.

Same graph, same scout, same merge, same compute. **Varying drafts by subgroup
beats varying them by instruction** (+0.144, p<.001). The graph is not the
failure; merge_v2's way of using it is. That is a far better result for the
project than the retracted version of this paragraph suggested.

Caveat, and it is the same trap as 12.6: this is ONE generation run scored by two
judges, not two independent runs. Judge-robustness is established;
run-robustness is not. The replication is cheap -- a `persona_merge` arm on the
`flat` or `sel` question set, no retrieval needed, judged with q38.

Until that lands, the claim is: *persona-based draft diversity improves Overton
coverage on both judges in one run; retrieval-augmented merging does not improve
it on either.*

### 15.3 Length does not explain it

The obvious confound: this judge penalises length (sections 2, 11.2), so a win
could just be a shorter answer. It is not.

| v11 arm | p50 words | in band | q38 score |
|---|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | 64 | 0.328 | 0.440 |
| `merge_v2_rand` | 335 | 0.000 | 0.353 |
| `persona_merge` | 424 | 0.061 | **0.500** |
| `merge_v2` | 665 | 0.000 | 0.356 |

Score is NOT monotone in length:

* `persona_merge` is LONGER than `merge_v2_rand` (424 vs 335) and scores +0.147
  higher.
* `persona_merge` is 6.6x longer than `baseline` and still beats it (+0.060).
* `merge_v2` at 665 words scores the same as `rand` at 335 -- doubling the
  length changed nothing.

persona_merge therefore wins while carrying a ~360-word penalty against an
instrument known to punish length. The +0.060 understates the content effect
rather than being produced by it.

This also disposes of a reading of section 14.1: baseline beating merge_v2 is
consistent with a pure length story, but persona beating baseline at 424 words
is not, so length alone cannot account for the ordering of the arms.

Still worth doing: per-question length regression within each arm, and a
length-matched persona arm. But the arm-level pattern is non-monotone, which is
what a length artifact cannot produce.
