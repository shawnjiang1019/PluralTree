# Findings, 2026-09-20

The headline result reverses under a better-calibrated judge, and the cause is
measurable: answer length. Everything else here is secondary to that.

Supersedes parts of [`findings_2026_09_15.md`](findings_2026_09_15.md) (§1, §12)
and [`established_claims.tex`](established_claims.tex). Job-by-job record in

## 1. The headline reverses under a different judge

Same 60 questions, same stored answers, two judges:

| condition | Qwen2.5-72B-AWQ | Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.4899 | 0.3899 |
| merge_v2 | **0.5589** | 0.2679 |
| merge_v2_flat | 0.5504 | 0.2711 |

Paired against baseline under the new judge: merge_v2 **−0.122**
(95% CI [−0.168, −0.074], p < 0.0001), flat −0.119 ([−0.164, −0.074]). Under the
old judge merge_v2 was **+0.069** ([+0.024, +0.113], p = 0.003). The intervals do
not overlap: a 0.19 swing in opposite directions on identical text.

## 2. Why: length, and where the benchmark was calibrated

**The judges disagree about length.**

| | ρ(words, coverage) | shortest 25% (55 w) | longest 25% (778 w) |
|---|---|---|---|
| old judge | +0.079 | 0.466 | 0.553 |
| new judge | −0.244 | 0.380 | 0.228 |
| humans (reference models) | −0.004 | — | — |

**And the human calibration covers a narrow band the systems never enter.**

| | p5 | p50 | p95 | max | within human p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| human-rated responses (n=28,992) | 66 | 88 | 97 | 106 | — |
| baseline | 30 | 65 | 116 | — | 85.6% |
| merge_v2 | 448 | 672 | 879 | — | **0.0%** |
| merge_v2_flat | 464 | 667 | 896 | — | **0.0%** |

Humans show no length effect (−0.004 pooled, +0.019 within question) but only
ever rated 66–106 word answers. merge_v2 answers are ~7x longer than the longest
response any human rated, so every judge verdict on them is extrapolation, and
two reasonable judges extrapolate oppositely.

## 3. Which judge to believe: the new one, on the axis that matters

Both validated against the same 300 held-out human ratings:

| | old judge | new judge | human ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| per-rating MAE | 0.527 | **0.523** | 0.592 |
| per-rating spearman | **0.653** | 0.626 | 0.673 |
| corr(rating, length) vs human −0.017 | — | **−0.017** | — |
| length-strata bias | +0.147, +0.147, +0.280, **+0.333** | +0.000, −0.053, −0.067, +0.080 | — |
| P(rating ≥ 4) vs human 0.650 | 0.747 | **0.647** | — |
| within-participant spearman | +0.059 | −0.014 | — |
| 8-model ranking spearman | 0.167 / 0.357 | 0.238, CI [−0.97, +0.92] | 0.574 (split-half) |

Per-rating accuracy is a wash; both sit near the human ceiling. The separation is
on the two axes that decide the headline:

- **Length bias.** The old judge over-rates the longest stratum by +0.333 of a
  rating point and the bias grows monotonically with length. The new judge's
  length correlation matches humans exactly.
- **The coverage threshold.** Coverage counts a cluster at mean rating >= 4. The
  old judge says >= 4 for 74.7% of ratings against humans' 65.0%; the new judge
  is at 64.7%.

So the negative verdict comes from the better-calibrated instrument on the
dimension in dispute. The 8-model ranking level cannot adjudicate anything: its
bootstrap CI spans [−0.97, +0.92], and human split-half reliability there is only
0.574.

**Both judges remain unvalidated above ~110 words.** The new judge simply has no
measured in-band length bias to extrapolate.

## 4. The length controls (running)

`len_ctrl`: `baseline, baseline_long, merge_v2, merge_v2_brief`, 3 rollouts,
scored under both judges.

- **`baseline_long`** — no retrieval, ~650 words (merge_v2's p50 is 672). Both
  arms long, so judge length bias cancels.
- **`merge_v2_brief`** — full retrieval and merge, final answer ~90 words, inside
  the human band. The only clean test of whether retrieved content helps.
  Implementation note: merge_v2's lossless guard asserts merged >= longest draft
  in words and positions, so a brief merge is flagged lossy and replaced by the
  concatenation fallback. The guard's floors are relaxed for this condition only.

Not a handicap: the benchmark's own reference models answered in ~88 words and
scored 3.7-3.8 with humans.

## 5. Selection ablations (measured with the OLD judge — now suspect)

All three perturbations of merge_v2's selection rule lose its entire gain:

| arm vs merge_v2 | delta | 95% CI | p | vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| spread | −0.085 | [−0.140, −0.031] | 0.002 | −0.016 (n.s.) |
| jsdiv | −0.059 | [−0.109, −0.010] | 0.017 | −0.021 (n.s.) |
| cover | −0.050 | [−0.106, +0.004] | 0.071 | +0.019 (n.s.) |

Manipulation checks passed on volume (4.85-4.90 forks) and, for jsdiv, on
disagreement (mean_js 0.0447 vs 0.0395). But **spread did not do what it was
built to do**: 1.67 distinct questions per row against merge_v2's 1.98 — it
changed branch granularity, not topic spread.

No measured property orders the losses: questions/row (flat injects 5.00 and
*ties*; spread 1.67 and loses), relevance (cover drops it most, −0.083, and loses
least), W, or JS. The parsimonious reading is that any substitution for
`argmax(rel^alpha * W)` returns to baseline, and the data cannot say which
property matters.

**Caveat added today:** all of these were scored by the length-biased judge on
600+ word answers, outside the validated band. They may be uninformative rather
than negative. Re-judging with the new judge is cheap and unrun.

## 6. RL gate: blocked by the instrument, not the reward

Stage 0 ran (60 questions x 8 samples, 7B base, plain prompts) and the gate
failed on the concordance check, not on headroom:

```
headroom          +0.176   PASS (union@8 0.544 vs within-answer 0.368)
flat groups        0.267   PASS (collapse was not the blocker)
judge vs itself    0.507   = CHANCE
reward concordance 0.228   (bar 0.503; ties on 51% of pairs; lambda=0 control 0.225)
```

Judged with all participants instead of 20: within 0.2835 -> union@8 0.4353
(+0.152), so the across-sample headroom is robust. But a judge that cannot rank
8 samples of ONE model above chance cannot validate any per-sample reward —
which also explains the old coverage reward (0.152), the new one (0.228), and
five failed routers. Note both judges are at chance here (+0.059 and −0.014
within-participant), so this is not fixed by the judge swap.

## 7. Geometry: both embeddings are boundary-saturated

`validate_divergence`, within-axis (the scale fork selection uses):

| | ATP | ISSP |
|---|---|---|
| geodesic vs JS | +0.755 | +0.086 |
| input features vs JS | +0.074 | +0.893 |
| mean JS (same-axis pairs) | 0.0154 | 0.0144 |

Cause: every node sits at the projection clamp, rho = 0.9999, 100% of nodes, on
both graphs. It is dimensional, not dynamical — the Tree-GRU tangent norm grows
like sqrt(d_hidden) (~7 at d=64) while `exp_map_zero` saturates past ~5. ATP
retains angular structure and still ranks disagreement; ISSP does not.

A fresh legacy ATP retrain shows saturation is also *progressive*: frac(rho>0.9)
0.965 -> 1.000 and pairwise distance sd 2.38 -> 1.75 over training, while val MRR
barely moves.

**Fix attempt 1 (scale only) failed**: ISSP retrained with `--tangent_scale -1`
still had p50 rho 0.9998, frac>0.9 0.844, and `depth_radius_rho` *fell* from
0.590 to 0.319. The reason is a feedback loop: `h_agg_tan` is the log map of the
children, whose norm diverges near the rim, so saturated children saturate the
parent and a constant factor only delays it.

**Fix attempt 2 (`--tangent_clip`)** bounds rho <= tanh(sqrt(c)*clip) regardless
of the children — 0.61 at clip 1.0, c 0.5. Implemented, not yet retrained.

Also unexplained: this legacy ATP retrain gets test MRR 0.2647, while the
codebase cites 0.456 for the ATP embedding. Resolve before either number is used.

## 8. What can be claimed now

**Weaker than yesterday.** "merge_v2 improves Overton coverage" is not currently
supportable: it holds under a judge that over-rates length and over-crosses the
coverage threshold, and reverses under one that matches humans on both. Neither
judge is validated at the lengths merge_v2 produces.

**Unchanged.** The across-sample headroom (+0.15 to +0.18), the benchmark's
inability to rank samples of one system, and the fact that no structural
component (curvature, hierarchy, divergence selection) explains anything.

**What would restore a positive claim.** `merge_v2_brief` beating `baseline`
inside the 66-106 word band, under both judges. That is the experiment now
running.

## 10. Cluster level: the mechanism survives the reversal (analysed 2026-09-23)

`cluster_overlap.py` on the three `*_q38_clusters.csv` dumps. §1–§4 compared
*mean* coverage; this compares the *sets*, which is where the mechanism lives.
330 clusters over 60 questions (5.5/question); baseline misses 2.93 of them per
question in every file.

| dump | arm | uniq/q | recovered | prev of uniq | prev of missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat | `merge_v2` | 0.25 | 8.5% | 0.274 | 0.455 |
| flat | `merge_v2_flat` | 0.32 | 10.8% | 0.257 | 0.455 |
| comp | `merge_v2_compressed` | 0.25 | 8.5% | 0.347 | 0.456 |

**The arms recover the minority end, and the new judge agrees.** `prev of uniq`
sits at 0.26–0.27 against 0.455 for what baseline missed: the clusters merge_v2
adds are roughly 60% the size of the average dropped cluster. This is the
mechanism OvertonBench exists to reward, it was never visible in the scalar
means, and it holds under the judge that reversed the headline.

**Compression preserves the covered set exactly.** `merge_v2_compressed` recovers
0.25 clusters/question at 8.5% — identical to the uncompressed arm — while its
mean score fell (§4). So the drop is the judge scoring the same viewpoints lower
for lack of depth, not the pipeline losing viewpoints. That separates two things
§1 could not: the *coverage mechanism* and the *depth premium*.

Not free, though: `prev of uniq` rises 0.274 → 0.347 under compression. What
survives the rewrite is the less-minority end of what merge_v2 finds, consistent
with the fixed-90 rewrite favouring enumerable majority positions. Still below
0.456, so the bias is attenuated rather than lost. Re-check on `compress_m`.

**The hierarchy null replicates at cluster level.** `merge_v2_flat` recovers
*more* than `merge_v2` (0.32 vs 0.25/q, 10.8% vs 8.5%) and is equally
minority-biased (0.257). Jaccard 0.619 between what the two arms add to baseline:
they largely find the same clusters. Tree structure contributes nothing here
either, now measured on sets rather than means.

**The ceiling is not a minority tail.** 155/330 (47.0%) of clusters are covered
by no arm in the flat dump, at mean relative prevalence **0.482** — at or above
the 0.455 of what baseline alone missed. These are mid-sized viewpoints, not
fringe ones, and no merge, router or reward in this family reaches them. That is
a bound on the method family, and it is a larger number than any effect measured
in this document.

Caveat: the three dumps carry different condition sets (3 arms / 2 arms /
baseline-only), so their "no arm covers" figures — 47.0%, 49.1%, 53.9% — measure
different unions and must not be read as a trend. Only the within-dump columns
compare.

**What this changes.** §8's "not currently supportable" stands for the mean-score
claim, but the set-level claim is stronger than the scalar one and was not
tested there: *the retrieved content reaches minority clusters baseline drops,
under both judges, at matched length*. Whether that converts into score is the
depth question, which is exactly what `baseline_long` and `compress_m` separate.

## 11. The gain/loss decomposition: what the net score was hiding (2026-09-23)

`cluster_overlap.py` reports only clusters an arm covers that baseline misses.
The score is `gain - loss`, and the loss column had never been computed. Adding
it reproduces every reported delta (flat +0.062 vs +0.069 reported, ISSP +0.047
vs +0.048, flat_q38 -0.126 vs -0.122, comp_q38 -0.178 vs -0.194), so the
decomposition is exact and the readings below are arithmetic, not inference.

### 11.1 Why minority recovery does not show up in the score

Under the new judge merge_v2 gains clusters at mean relative prevalence **0.274**
and loses them at **0.601**. It trades majority clusters for minority ones at
about one-for-two. OvertonBench weights every cluster equally, so that trade is
negative by construction: the recovered minority cluster earns exactly what the
dropped majority cluster cost. **The metric is prevalence-blind, and the
mechanism this project is built on is a prevalence trade.** No amount of better
retrieval fixes that; it is a property of the scoring rule.

This is the cleanest statement of the gap between what §10 measured (the arms
reach the minority end) and what §1 measured (the score fell).

### 11.2 The reversal is a threshold effect, not displacement

`flat` and `flat_q38` are the SAME responses scored by two judges:

| judge | gain/q | loss/q | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-72B-AWQ | 0.53 | 0.19 | +0.062 |
| Qwen3.8-27B | 0.28 | **1.00** | -0.126 |

Gain halved, loss rose 5x, with zero change to the text. Same pattern on the
compressed arm (-0.009 -> -0.178, loss 0.64 -> 1.30). merge_v2 covers many
positions shallowly, so when the >=4 bar is applied more strictly it has far more
marginal clusters to lose than baseline does. **The depth premium lives in the
loss column**, and it is not content displacement -- the pipeline never dropped
those viewpoints, the judge stopped counting them.

### 11.3 The selection ablations were never null

Same run, same judge, identical net scores, completely different ledgers:

| arm | gain/q | loss/q | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| `merge_v2` (spread run) | 0.53 | 0.17 | +0.065 |
| `merge_v2_cover` | **0.90** | 0.53 | +0.065 |
| `merge_v2_spread` | 0.81 | 0.60 | +0.037 |
| `merge_v2` (jsdiv run) | 0.53 | 0.12 | +0.083 |
| `merge_v2_jsdiv` | **0.83** | 0.36 | +0.083 |
| `merge_v2_divrand` | 0.69 | 0.17 | **+0.092** |
| `merge_v2_rand` (v12) | 0.81 | 0.38 | +0.077 |
| `persona_merge` (v12) | 0.90 | 0.53 | +0.065 |

`cover` recovers 70% more of what baseline missed than merge_v2 does; `jsdiv`
57% more. They tie on net only because they lose proportionally more. **"Any
substitution for argmax(rel^alpha * W) returns to baseline" (job log, WHAT IT
ADDS UP TO) is false.** The selection mechanisms move substantial mass in both
directions and cancel.

Correction to §5 and to the job log: those arms are not null, they are
differently shaped at equal net.

### 11.4 Loss is the untouched lever

Every intervention so far has targeted gain (better retrieval, better ranking,
better selection). Nothing has targeted loss. `cover`'s gain at `merge_v2`'s loss
rate is net **+0.127** -- double the largest effect ever measured in this project.

The concrete version: a merge guard that requires the final answer to retain the
positions a baseline answer would have covered, rather than only maximising
retrieved positions. Currently the merge optimises breadth over injected content
and is free to drop whatever the model would have said unaided. Untested.

### 11.5 The gain is 72B-specific

On the 7B generator merge_v2 is negative under both judges:

| file | gain/q | loss/q | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| `7b` | 0.12 | 1.65 | -0.285 |
| `7b_j72` | 0.45 | 0.80 | -0.062 |

Not recorded anywhere before this. Any claim about injection helping is a claim
about the 72B generator only, and the RL plan (7B policy) inherits this.
