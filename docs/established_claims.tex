\section{Instrument}

All coverage numbers come from an LLM judge whose own validation is unresolved.
Its aggregate agreement with human ratings is $\rho = 0.167$, against a ceiling
of $\sqrt{0.574} \approx 0.76$ set by the benchmark's own human split-half
reliability ($0.574$ after the Spearman--Brown correction). With only eight
reference models the sampling error at that level is very wide (split-half range
$[-0.17, +0.83]$), so the ranking-level check can neither validate nor invalidate
a judge. Read every item below through that.

\section{Established}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsc{merge\_v2} $>$ baseline replicates on the reference embedding:
    $+0.039$, $+0.050$ ($p{=}0.014$), $+0.048$ ($p{=}0.024$) across independent
    generations at 72B. On freshly trained embeddings it is marginal in both
    geometry arms ($+0.040$, $p{=}0.062$; $+0.045$, $p{=}0.061$; three
    rollouts), so embedding-training variance may matter.
  \item The gain transfers to a second, independently built survey graph. On one
    ISSP module, restricted to the $34$ of $60$ questions whose anchors resolve,
    \textsc{merge\_v2} gains $+0.048$ (95\% CI $[+0.005, +0.094]$, $p{=}0.031$);
    on the $26$ unresolved questions, where the arm runs without forks, the
    difference is $-0.008$ ($p{=}0.65$). Pooled over all 60 the effect dilutes to
    $+0.024$ ($p{=}0.11$), as attenuation predicts.
  \item The retrieval instrument is valid. Within an axis --- the only comparison
    fork selection makes --- the learned hyperbolic distance ranks the
    Jensen--Shannon divergence between subgroups' answer distributions at
    $\rho = +0.755$ (2{,}027 axes; pooled $+0.529$; shuffled null $-0.000$),
    while the encoder's own input features rank it at only $+0.074$. The encoder
    recovers ordering its inputs do not expose.
  \item The merge is faithful: median retention $1.0$, with the semantic guard
    firing on 1 of 60 merges. Where coverage is lost at 7B, the loss is in draft
    quality, not compression.
  \item CAD at 7B is monotone in $\alpha$ across five values
    ($0.352, 0.327, 0.099, 0.047, 0.042$ for $\alpha = -0.5..{+}0.5$): the
    strength of context influence controls coverage, and suppressing it helps.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ruled out as drivers of coverage}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Curvature.} At three rollouts the hyperbolic and Euclidean arms
    converge ($+0.040$ vs $+0.045$; $\Delta\Delta = +0.005$, 95\% CI
    $[-0.040, +0.057]$, $p{=}0.88$), a well-powered null, even though the
    hyperbolic encoder fits the tree markedly better (tree-distance $\rho$ $0.64$
    vs $0.29$; sibling ratio $0.65$ vs $0.08$). An earlier one-rollout run
    appeared to separate the arms ($\Delta\Delta$ at $p{=}0.094$); it did not
    replicate.
  \item \emph{Hierarchy.} Flat retrieval --- no anchor, no descent, nearest
    opinion leaves paired into pseudo-forks --- ties \textsc{merge\_v2}
    ($+0.010$ in flat's favour, 95\% CI $[-0.011, +0.031]$, $p{=}0.34$), with
    fork volume matched ($5.00$ vs $4.85$) and lower divergence ($11.9$ vs
    $13.7$). Any hierarchy benefit is bounded near $+0.01$.
  \item \emph{Per-question routing.} On 60 questions no feature set beats the
    trivial always-inject policy, which recovers $+0.525$ of the oracle gap;
    graph features recover $-0.040$ and the full causal set $-0.178$, below
    length alone ($+0.118$). Five earlier routers failed the same way.
\end{itemize}

\section{Not established}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{That divergence selection matters.} Random-pair (\textsc{divrand})
    and distribution-ranked (\textsc{jsmax}) selection arms have not reported.
    The disagreement being ranked is small in absolute terms: mean
    $\mathrm{JS} = 0.0154$ bits over same-axis subgroup pairs, so the most
    divergent available pair is often barely divergent.
  \item \emph{That fork content is load-bearing.} The matched irrelevant-fork
    control (\textsc{rand}) was confounded in every prior run.
  \item \emph{That the gain is not length.} Injected answers run roughly five
    times longer than baseline's and no length-matched baseline has been run.
  \item \emph{The reward.} Reward--judge concordance within question is $0.15$
    against chance $0.5$; the reward scores survey positions, the judge scores
    participant clusters, and no threshold reconciles them. The group-diversity
    reward replacing it is gated on the same statistic and has not yet been
    measured.
  \item \emph{CAD's magnitude.} The no-forks reference ($0.394$) uses a different
    instruction from the CAD arms, so $0.394 \to 0.099$ conflates the fork block
    with the instruction. The $\alpha = -1$ arm would separate them and has not
    been run.
  \item \emph{The 7B collapse's size.} At 7B \textsc{merge\_v2} falls
    $0.17$--$0.26$ below baseline, but measured with a 7B judge; the 72B re-judge
    is pending.
\end{itemize}

\section{Known trade-off}

Across-sample diversity (INFINITY-CHAT): \textsc{merge\_v2} is \emph{less}
diverse than baseline on every metric, losing 84--89 of 100 queries
($p \approx 0$). It trades across-sample diversity for within-answer coverage.
Retrieval resolved on 71 of the 100 queries.

\section{Where coverage is lost}

Of the viewpoint clusters the benchmark scores, $29.7\%$ (ATP) and $45.4\%$
(ISSP) are covered by no arm at all, and those clusters are smaller than average
(relative size $0.278$ on ISSP). The clusters an arm \emph{does} recover beyond
baseline are larger than a typical miss ($0.53$ vs $0.45$), so no arm is reaching
the minority end the benchmark exists to reward. Which stage loses them ---
retrieval, drafting, merging or judging --- is the question
\texttt{scripts/analysis/coverage\_funnel.py} was built to answer.
